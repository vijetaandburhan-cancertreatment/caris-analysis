# Findings and validation starting points

Research snapshot: 5 October 2026. Source: nine supplied Caris bulk DNA/RNA files for accession TN26-279853. Use this current table to inspect or challenge each inference. The [one-page handoff](../reports/Caris-DNA-RNA-research-handoff-TN26-279853-2026-10-05-v2.pdf) is a dated overview; read its [current clarifications and errata](CURRENT_STATUS_AND_ERRATA.md) alongside it. These results do not establish a diagnosis, treatment response, survival estimate or clinical eligibility.

The entries distinguish **vendor calls**, **research observations** and **model hypotheses**. Computational checks were internal to an AI-assisted workflow; they are not external peer review or laboratory confirmation. See [interpretation limits](LIMITATIONS.md) and the [glossary](GLOSSARY.md).

## DNA/RNA consistency and alignment sensitivity

This work found broad allele compatibility **with unresolved local exceptions**. It is not a validated sample-identity test or contamination estimate.

The frozen population-selected panel contains 165,782 SNPs on six chromosomes. Of 4,015 sites with at least 20 qualifying A/C/G/T query-name units in both original BAMs, 2,873 have a ≥98% dominant DNA REF/ALT allele. Original RNA retains that allele at ≥90% at 2,860/2,873 sites and at ≥98% at 2,760/2,873. No callable site flips to ≥98% of the opposite REF/ALT allele. A coordinate-first ≥50-kb-spaced subset retains the same allele at ≥90% at all 120 callable DNA-dominant sites. Selection and coverage constraints prevent an identity probability.

After separate full-library RNA alignment, the same fixed denominator remains 2,873: **2,805** retain both depth ≥20 and same-allele fraction ≥90%; 59 lose depth qualification and 9 remain callable below 90%. At the ≥98% threshold the count is 2,721. A fraction-only count must not replace these depth-qualified results.

Five of six strong original discrepancies persist, including two PABPC1 sites only 13 bases apart that share read support. APOD changes with alignment: 26/27 originally C-supporting names now support DNA-expected G, while one fails matched filters. This demonstrates mapping dependence, not proof that the new placement is biologically correct. None of these exceptions has been promoted to a tumor mutation or target.

Read the [population-panel report](../reports/Caris-DNA-RNA-population-panel-addendum-2026-10-05.txt), followed by the [later alignment comparison](../reports/Caris-final-alignment-comparison-2026-10-05.txt). The earlier population report's limited-alignment analysis was not evaluable; the later full-library comparison is separate completed work. Detailed [population evidence](../evidence/population-panel/) and [alignment evidence](../evidence/alignment-comparison/) preserve counts, denominators, exceptions and internal cross-checks.

## Useful next work for an external validator

1. Independently reconstruct the exact coding alleles, transcript translations and mutant/reference peptide windows from the original inputs; assess alternative alignments and transcript conventions, not only agreement with the existing scripts.
2. Re-examine CN/LOH using calibrated vendor segments and purity/ploidy if available. Existing counts alone cannot distinguish the competing copy-number explanations.
3. Establish matched-normal variant status and high-resolution HLA independently before making somatic, hereditary or HLA-restriction claims.
4. For antigen work, distinguish binding, endogenous presentation and selective recognition. Preserve the WT and near-self controls; peptide-pulsing alone would not establish tumor presentation.
5. Revisit ambiguous junctions and PABPC1 read origins with an independently specified reference/aligner strategy. Report changed placements, coverage failures and unresolved alternatives; do not silently label them corrections.

A useful contribution should identify the repository commit, exact inputs and references, commands/settings, controls, numerical differences and interpretation limits. Replicating the calculation is valuable; a different biological assay is needed to validate the underlying biological claim.
