BAP1 antigen interpretation: what the stronger sequence evidence does and does not add
1 October 2026

The exact local DNA/RNA sequence, canonical splice linkage and two correlated
binding-model releases support carrying the BAP1 frameshift into expert
experimental triage. They do not establish a vaccine-ready target. The observed
mutant RNA is more informative than simply labeling the gene BAP1 or the change
frameshift: the conditional product contains only seven altered amino acids,
not a long new reading frame. RASA1 contains only two, and the LATS1 insertion
creates an immediate stop with no altered tail in the selected transcript.

An early stop is not, by itself, a reason to discard a candidate. A cell-model
study showed that premature-stop-containing transcripts can supply MHC-I
peptides even when full-length protein production is curtailed. This is a
mechanistic possibility, not evidence that these particular BAP1 peptides are
produced. The requirement is direct peptide/HLA or functional T-cell evidence,
not necessarily detection of a stable truncated BAP1 protein on a routine assay.
Source: Apcher et al., PNAS 2011,
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3136330/

The larger frameshift/NMD literature gives a reason to investigate expressed
frameshifts even in low-TMB cancers. Litchfield et al. found associations between
expressed frameshifts and immunotherapy benefit in small melanoma cohorts, and
reviewed experimentally reactive frameshift peptides. Longer altered tails
were enriched among reactive examples, although the comparison was small and
confounded by how many peptides were tested. These data do not provide a
response probability for this patient, mesothelioma, the BAP1 seven-residue
tail or a particular vaccine. RNA expression should not be converted into a
measured NMD-escape rate or a protein-production claim.
Source: Nature Communications 2020,
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17526-5

Normal-reference sequence comparison

A complete equal-length search across 104,760 GENCODE37 protein records found
no exact, one-substitution or two-substitution normal-reference neighbor for
IEERKGLYL. For EERKGLYL there were no exact or one-substitution matches and 24
distinct two-substitution neighbors. This is a bounded sequence observation,
not proof of tumor specificity, immunogenicity or safety. It excludes neither
normal population variants nor noncanonical translation, different-length
cross-recognition or unrelated peptides recognized by the same T-cell receptor.

The 24 neighboring eight-mers were scored locally against HLA-B*50:01 with the
same two affinity-model releases. Several also have favorable predicted
affinity. That is expected for some self peptides and does not demonstrate
cross-reactivity; HLA binding and T-cell recognition are different questions.
The sequences can serve as an optional experimental control list if Michael's
team develops a cognate T-cell assay. They should not be described as predicted
toxic off-targets, and this comparison should not alone select or reject the
nine-mer versus eight-mer.

Priority order for a research team

1. Establish the exact variant in a current tumor specimen and normal-DNA
   comparator, and confirm high-resolution blood HLA. The July brain specimen
   cannot establish that every current extracranial site shares the target.
2. Independently review the mutant transcript and local phasing, and retain
   both overlapping BAP1 peptides as one mutation hypothesis.
3. Assess actual tumor HLA-B*50:01 expression/retention and peptide presentation
   or T-cell recognition with appropriate normal/self controls. Positive bulk
   RNA HLA typing is not proof of malignant-cell allele retention.
4. Only then determine whether the candidate belongs in a development program.
   These research observations do not replace standard treatment, establish
   clinical-trial eligibility or predict survival.
