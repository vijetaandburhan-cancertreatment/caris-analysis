Targeted RNA splice/allele comparison — 5 October 2026

The full-primary-reference re-alignment preserves BAP1's exact expressed deletion and LATS1's insertion. It adds confidence in local RNA sequence support and splice context; it does not establish complete mutant isoforms, protein production or therapeutic response. These are two analyses of the same specimen/read pool, not independent biological replication.

Same filters in both alignments: primary mapped non-QC-fail/non-duplicate/non-supplementary records, MAPQ>=20 and NH=1 when present; exact variant haplotype with 10 bp flanks and BQ>=20; splice junctions with 12 immediately adjacent reference-exact, BQ>=20 bases on each side. Query-name/RG collapses mates, not UMI molecules. Raw query names were matched across the single original/new library despite differing RG labels.

BAP1: original/new alternate fragments 246/246, reference 53/52, other 12/12. All 310 informative names present in both runs have the same allele classification. The mutation-containing exon has strong annotated flanking junction support: lower genomic flank 218→214 names and upper 461→459. Alternate-linked names remain 40 and 135; mutation-plus-junction on the same read remains 28 and 133. This is local evidence that deletion-bearing RNA participates in annotated splicing around this exon; it is not a full-length transcript or presentation assay.

The junction skipping the entire 133 bp mutation-containing BAP1 exon has only 2 query-name fragments in each alignment, versus the much larger flanking-junction counts above. It is already annotated in GENCODE37 transcript ENST00000490917.1, labelled nonsense_mediated_decay. Those reads cannot be assigned to the deletion allele because they skip its location. These descriptive counts are not calibrated isoform fractions, and the observation is not evidence of a tumor-specific functional rescue.

Two stronger BAP1 junctions absent from GENCODE37's BAP1 transcript list remain visible in both alignments: chr3:52402871→52403182 (0-based intron interval), 34→32 names, and 52404586→52405764, 32→32. Both have strand-correct GT-AG motifs. Relative to MANE Select ENST00000460680.6, their introns include 45 and 185 reference CDS nucleotides respectively. They are downstream of, and unphased to, the early frameshift. The records are retained as minor alternative-junction observations; they do not establish cancer specificity, a full RNA/protein isoform, rescue, a new antigen or drug sensitivity. Lack of annotation in one GENCODE release does not establish novelty.

LATS1: original/new alternate 4/4 and reference 2/2. Each of the two annotated flanking junctions retains 2 same-read alternate-plus-junction observations. Counts remain small. This supports the previously defined local RNA insertion but cannot establish somatic status, protein loss or Hippo/TEAD biology. LATS2 has 10 qualifying junctions in both runs; its separate Gly960Arg allele evidence is outside this three-edit counter and must not be inferred from its 'uncovered' state here.

RASA1: original/new alternate 75/61, reference 46/46. This net 14 decrease is 15 previously supporting names lost and 1 gained. All 15 losses replace a short 11–14bp deletion-bearing leading alignment block with soft clipping, removing the strict left 10 bp anchor; 8 also become MAPQ 0/NH 5 and 1 MAPQ 3/NH 2. Six remain uniquely mapped but lack the required anchor. The gained name changes in the opposite direction. Both mates' forward sequences and qualities are identical across runs for all 16 names. This is alignment/filter sensitivity, not disappearance of the mutation or a biological change. Version, reference and settings differ, so the cause cannot be assigned solely to sparse indexing.

Independent validation: the original counter's 101 junction rows and the new counter's 102 rows, with all total/alternate-linked/same-read counts and allele states, were independently reproduced using explicit CIGAR walking. The six original software controls and 25 additional independent synthetic controls passed. Computational agreement is not clinical assay validation. Tables preserve all junctions, including weak/unannotated ones, and the exact same-read/name-level evidence.

Most useful next laboratory evidence: orthogonal confirmation and matched normal for candidate alleles; if complete BAP1 isoforms or NMD escape become important, targeted full-length/long-read RNA or suitably designed laboratory cDNA work is needed. No new tissue, matched normal or treatment-response timepoint was analyzed here.
