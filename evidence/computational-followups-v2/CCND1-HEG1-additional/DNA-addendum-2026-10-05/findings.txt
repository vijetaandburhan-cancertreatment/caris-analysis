CCND1::HEG1 — bounded DNA addendum, 5 October 2026

The exact RNA-derived joined sequence was not found in the bounded DNA records. There are, however, two nonduplicate DNA query-name families with related, non-exact split sequences, plus one duplicate-marked copy. These warrant an unresolved DNA-level hint, not an entirely negative DNA result and not a DNA-confirmed fusion. The existing RNA audit remains unchanged. Neither evidence establishes a somatic rearrangement, a translated driver, or treatment relevance.

Scope and exact sequence check
The indexed original Caris DNA BAM was fetched only around the two proposed loci, including the union of the 18 microhomology-equivalent breakpoints plus 1,000 bases on each side: chr11 [69,650,216, 69,652,234) and chr3 [125,012,571, 125,014,589), GRCh38 zero-based half-open. All 18 placements represent one joined-sequence hypothesis, not independent events. The 50- and 100-base markers around each placement were checked in both orientations: 72 distinct markers, with support collapsed by query name plus read group across placements and mates.

The two region fetches returned 34,574 and 9,905 alignments. Prioritized mate checks covered all 458 selected names without reaching the 500-name cap: 454 mates were already present and four alignments were added. The saved bounded BAM contains 44,483 distinct alignments, 22,246 query-name/read-group units, and 19,611 strict alignments. Strict means mapped primary, MAPQ at least 20, not secondary, supplementary, duplicate-marked or QC-failed. Nonexclusive flags across the saved records include 24,684 duplicate-marked, 165 secondary, 21 unmapped and 45 MAPQ below 20 records.

There were zero exact 50- or 100-base marker matches at minimum BQ20 or BQ30, both in the strict set and when all flags were retained. Independent recount also found zero even without a base-quality threshold. This tests the exact reference-derived RNA model; it does not exclude related sequences carrying indels or substitutions. In particular, the two linked sequence families below differ from that model, and their 99- and 89-base stored sequences cannot contain a 100-base marker.

Related DNA split alignments
Three query names link HEG1 and CCND1 through SA/secondary alignments; two are nonduplicate and one is duplicate-marked. Their HEG1 primary alignments have MAPQ60. The apparent cross-chromosome mate links occur in secondary CCND1 records: the actual primary mates both align to HEG1, at identical starts, with identical stored sequences. They are not additional independently spanning discordant pairs. The two mates fully overlap after orientation correction. Two different sequence/start families remain; neither a sequence family nor a query name is a UMI-validated independent molecule.

• Family DNA_Q2: an 89-base sequence, one nonduplicate name. HEG1 primary 69M20S begins at chr3:125013526; the CCND1 secondary placement is 53H36M at chr11:69651220. The placements overlap 16 query bases. Reverse complement aligns across the model with 86 bases aligned and a three-base insertion, no substitutions. The displayed first-best alignment places TCT at model index233; repeat-equivalent indel placement is not a unique genomic allele definition. These alignments fit a near-joined sequence better than the nominated ordinary references. The first-best score placements are descriptive, not a validated genomic breakpoint or HGVS variant. The repeat-rich context, tiny number of distinct start patterns, completely overlapping mates, non-exact relationship to the RNA sequence, and lack of matched normal or orthogonal structural confirmation prevent a firm biological interpretation. DNA-library chimeras and other assay artifacts remain possible; this observation does not establish that the RNA event is exclusively an RNA-library phenomenon either.

Ordinary-reference alternative check
The two unique DNA sequences were run in both orientations through the unchanged RNA-audit nomination/scoring scripts, with all nine reference-derived/synthetic controls retained. The search scanned the 3,099,750,718-base primary GRCh38 assembly (194 contigs) and all 234,485 GENCODE37 transcripts (384,229,184 bases), then scored 17,722 nominated windows (6,921 genomic and 10,801 transcript). The 17,722 windows include controls; 187 windows came from the two DNA families themselves (152 genomic and 35 transcript, across both orientations). No 20,000-hit seed cap or 20,000-window query cap was reached. Both best ordinary alternatives remained HEG1; there was no equally good clean contiguous or normal-transcript explanation in the nominated set.

Nomination uses disjoint 19-base seeds plus a terminal 19-base seed, both orientations, with 20-base origin slack and nearby-origin merging. For these 99- and 89-base queries, respectively five and four disjoint seeds ensure that up to four or three substitutions leave at least one seed intact. This is not an exhaustive gapped mapper and does not guarantee retrieval across arbitrary indels, alternate haplotypes, structural polymorphisms or missing references. Scores use the previously verified full-query physical-window-end conventions: match +2, mismatch −4, internal gap −6−(length−1), free reference end overhangs and query end gap cost −2/base only at a physical reference-window boundary. Scores are relative alignment scores, not MAPQ, likelihoods or biological probabilities. Query coordinates/BQs here refer to the stored BAM sequence, not independently recovered original DNA FASTQs.

All seven ordinary-parent controls, including three substitutions, a one-base insertion, a two-base deletion, a canonical MET splice and an annotated INS-IGF2 transcript, recovered their true normal source windows and expected scores. The synthetic joined sequence scored 322 versus best normal 148. The CGG-repeat control had thousands of candidate placements and was not treated as unique. These are method controls, not clinical structural-variant sensitivity or specificity estimates.

Coverage and limitations
Strict BQ20 aligned base coverage across the 18 equivalent positions was 4,715–4,830 query-name/read-group units at CCND1 and 338–394 at HEG1. The retained 25-base anchors had 3,631–4,127 contiguous aligned units at CCND1 (3,432–4,074 reference-exact) and 81–244 at HEG1 (69–238 reference-exact). For retained 50-base anchors the corresponding counts were 2,608–2,726 (2,543–2,667 reference-exact) and 71–183 (66–171 reference-exact). These establish coverage of the examined exonic/retained bases only. They are not variant-allele-frequency denominators; overlapping mates with conflicting bases can contribute to more than one base category, and no purity/capture calibration was performed.

Capture or coverage of all potentially relevant intronic DNA was not established. A genomic breakpoint need not equal the observed RNA join, and both-unmapped fragments or distant uncaptured breakpoints were not searched. This was a bounded index fetch, not a whole-DNA structural-variant analysis. The original BAM's size and modification time were unchanged; the prior RNA manifest hash was unchanged. No new clinical classification or treatment conclusion follows.
